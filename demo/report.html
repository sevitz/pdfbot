How the term went

The first half of term was dominated by one long adventure novel, which is where most of the nautical vocabulary came from: starboard, rigging, becalmed and quay all arrived in the same fortnight. Those words were hard at first because none of them turn up in everyday conversation, but they were also the ones that stuck best once they had been met three or four times in the story itself.

After half term the reading shifted to shorter non-fiction about volcanoes and the deep sea. The words from those books were more abstract (dormant, pressure, erosion) and the typed answers show it: plenty of yellow grades where the idea was right but the explanation stopped short. A yellow is a good sign, not a bad one. It means the word is on its way in.

Reading the grades. Green means the meaning was explained correctly. Yellow means the idea was close but incomplete. Orange means a related word was described instead. Red means the answer missed, and the word comes back sooner next time.

What comes next

This section starts on a new page because its heading carries data-pdfbot-break. Next term the plan is to mix the two kinds of reading from the start, so that story words and fact words arrive side by side, and to bring back any word that has not been asked for more than three weeks.

The quiz will also start offering a hint after a red answer, on the principle that getting a word right with help is more useful than getting it wrong twice. Words that are answered green three times in a row will drop to a slow rotation, so the daily quiz stays short.

One thing to try at home. When a new word turns up at the dinner table, ask for it in a sentence before asking what it means. Using a word is easier than defining it, and it usually comes first.
